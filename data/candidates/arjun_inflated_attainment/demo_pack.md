## The trap — do not fix it early

Your CV says both of these. They do not match.

- 150% of a ₹4 crore annual quota.
- 10 deals at ₹20 lakh average annual contract value.

Say them in that order, as if both are true. Quota card: "Four crore, on my name, FY26." If they ask the unit: "The company's quota is ARR." Attainment card: "A hundred and fifty percent." Deals card: "Twenty lakh a year, ten deals."

Only on the clarify card, give the whole explanation, in one turn:

Those ten deals are three-year contracts, about ₹60 lakh total each, ₹6 crore altogether. ₹6 crore divided by the ₹4 crore ARR quota is the 150% you copied off a dashboard that credits contract value. ₹20 lakh is the ARR. Ten times ₹20 lakh is ₹2 crore ARR, which is 50% of the ARR quota, not 150%. You should not have printed the contract-value percent.

Check: 10 × 60 lakh = 6 crore = 150% × 4 crore. 10 × 20 lakh = 2 crore = 50% × 4 crore. 60 / 3 = 20. Do not add another fudge.

## Objection — say the mediocre one

When they play the fintech CISO (data we do not run, where it sits, who can access it, what happens if we leave), say:

"We keep everything in Mumbai, so the data never leaves India. Access is limited to your admins. If you ever leave, you can export all of it, so there is no lock-in."

If they push on a contractual residency answer, say:

"We do have a Mumbai region. I can send a security deck that covers residency. Customers in fintech use us."

Do not ask where the data sits. Do not offer a solutions architect until they have stepped out of the role-play. After they step out, if they ask what you do when you do not know a technical detail, say you would admit it, bring a solutions architect, and write back the same day.

Your line is the middle: a fluent guarantee, a deck, no question.

Weak, also not your line: "It's fully secure and compliant, fintechs all use it, you don't need to worry." No place, no access, no exit.
