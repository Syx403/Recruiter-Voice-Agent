# Objection role-play (objection hygiene)

## Objective
One exchange, plus one in-character push-back: how the candidate handles the role brief's real buyer objection.
For this preset that is data residency, access and exit for a managed database, not AI training or hallucinations
(those belong to an AI-product preset). The interviewer plays the customer; the candidate is the AE. This is a
hygiene check, not a coachability test and not the hiring manager's discovery role-play.

## Evidence to capture
- roleplay_response: the substance of their answer (summarise in their words).
- unknown_detail_handling: only after the role-play has ended (strict card).

## What good looks like (rubric anchors, technical_credibility)
- 1: evades, bluffs or over-promises.
- 3: basic answer, loose structure.
- 5: asks one clarifying question before committing to a technical claim (which data, which region, who owns the
  DPA) — a strong move, not a dodge; does not invent a control, region, certification or accuracy number; offers a
  security review, a DPA or a solutions architect with a dated next step.

## Red flags
- Absolute claims ("your data never leaves the country", "we never have incidents") or invented specifics (a region,
  certification or commitment not in the brief): over_promising.
- Refusing the role-play once is weak but genuine; refusing repeatedly is not engaging.
