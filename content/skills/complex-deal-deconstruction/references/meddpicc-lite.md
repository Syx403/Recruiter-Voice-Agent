# MEDDPICC, three letters only

For the reasoning step. Do not read this aloud. Do not add a question, a field, or a competency. Champion, competition, and paper process are the only letters here. Economic buyer, the blocker, and the customer number are already fields on the deal card.

## Champion

A champion is not the economic buyer. A champion has influence in the account and will advance the deal when the rep is not in the room. Hear it in the answer you already have. Did someone introduce the person who could say no, and did that someone do something after the meeting, or did the rep only have a friendly user? A title with no act is not a champion. Force Management's public MEDDPICC description treats the champion as a person with power and influence who sells internally. Do not cite a private certification manual, and do not ask "are you my champion?"

## Competition

Competition includes a named vendor, an internal build, and doing nothing. Doing nothing is a normal SaaS outcome, not a weak answer. "They chose on price," with no name and no moment, is thin. Do not require the candidate to disparage an incumbent or invent another vendor's price.

## Paper process

Paper process is what happens after a verbal yes: a security questionnaire, legal or an MSA, procurement, a purchase order, a signature. A small commercial deal may be a questionnaire and one signer. Do not mark that as thin. A deal near the top of the role's deal-size band, or any enterprise deal, with a verbal yes and no idea what happened next, is a gap. If they already said it, keep it on `main_blocker` or `own_actions`. Do not create a paper-process field.

## Scale

A VP Engineering who can say no is a valid buyer on a small commercial deal. "No separate CFO" is not a miss when the deal value is small. One contact and no security step on a large deal is a gap. Do not apply an enterprise checklist to a small commercial logo, and do not accept a commercial-style single signer on an enterprise deal at a bank.
