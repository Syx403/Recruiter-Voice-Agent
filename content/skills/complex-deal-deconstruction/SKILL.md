# Deal deconstruction: a new logo they opened, and a loss

## Objective
This role hires for repeatable new logos in a commercial segment, not for one enterprise outlier. Get one deal that
looks like a normal quarter for the candidate, from how the first meeting happened to the customer's outcome, and
what the candidate personally did. Then one lost deal.

## Evidence to capture
- first_meeting_source: own outbound, SDR, inbound or partner.
- economic_buyer: who could say no after the main contact said yes. On a small commercial deal one technical buyer
  can be the whole process: "one person decided" plus the deal size is a complete answer, not a thin one. On a deal
  near USD 100k+ with no finance, security or procurement step, the process is a real gap.
- main_blocker; own_actions (what THE CANDIDATE did, not "we"); deal_value (currency, annual or full contract).
- team_on_deal: who else was on it (SE, manager, SDR, partner) and what would not have happened without the
  candidate. (Replaces a contribution percentage, which reconciles to nothing.)
- customer_outcome: what changed for the customer in numbers (cost, downtime, release speed, risk). If they never
  quantified it, that is the answer; do not infer one.
- loss_review: what kind of loss (named competitor, no decision, price, product gap, champion left) and the point
  where they would act differently. The loss question stands alone: it may come before the won deal.

## Follow-up priority
1. own_actions. 2. first_meeting_source. 3. economic_buyer, scaled to the deal size. 4. customer_outcome.
5. main_blocker, deal_value, team_on_deal.

## What good looks like (rubric anchors)
deal_execution — 1: cannot name the roles or their own actions. 3: main roles named; buyer, blockers and own
actions thin. 5: clearly reconstructs who decided, the blocker and their own specific actions, at the depth the
deal size calls for.
outcome_orientation — 1: features only. 3: business results mentioned occasionally. 5: naturally ties the deal to a
quantified customer outcome. No customer-outcome evidence means null, not 3.
ownership_learning — 1: blames others. 3: some reflection, not specific. 5: names their own controllable mistake
and what they would change.

## Red flags
- Implausible speed or scale (a "complex" enterprise deal closed in a day): low effort or inflation.
- Motion mismatch: a claimed commercial motion whose only story is one multi-quarter enterprise outlier, or a
  claimed enterprise motion whose deals are all small monthly plans. A mismatch, not a moral judgement.
- Only "we" after being asked what they did: evasive. The loss is entirely someone else's fault: blames_others.
